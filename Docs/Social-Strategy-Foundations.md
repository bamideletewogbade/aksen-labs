# Social media as a foundation, not a channel

Started 14 September 2026. A first-principles working document for Aksen Labs.
Nigeria and Ghana are the primary markets; the reasoning starts global and then
lands locally. It is written to be argued with and extended, not followed.

## 1. The uncomfortable starting position

Any strategy has to begin from what is actually true today rather than what we
intend.

- The pipeline holds zero opportunities.
- One project exists (TFS), and the site describes it as proposed and
  illustrative, explicitly not evidence of a completed delivery.
- CV Forge is built and has no public address.
- The site itself states that examples are not proof of past results.

So a content strategy built on "look at our results" would be a lie this month.
That constraint is the whole design problem, and it points at the answer.

## 2. What is genuinely rare here

Aksen runs its own business on the thing it sells. The workspace has a lead
pipeline, an agent desk, an approvals queue, an audit trail, a research agent
that reads public company information, and a project system tracking
deliverables, decisions and risks. It is used for real work.

Almost no agency in Accra or Lagos can show that. The common competitor is a
deck and a thin wrapper over a chat model.

**So the content asset is the machine, not the case studies.** Document the
system that runs the company. That converts the weakness in section 1 into the
differentiator: we cannot yet show ten delivered clients, but we can show
something almost nobody will show, which is the inside of a working system.

It is also nearly free to produce. The commit messages and design documents in
this repository are already long-form explanations of real decisions, written
as a byproduct of the work. Raw material exists before any content work starts.

## 3. Where the obvious AI-and-social combination is a trap

The commodity version is "use AI to write the posts." Reject it, for three
reasons that compound:

1. The feed is already saturated with generated text, and audiences are getting
   better at spotting and discounting it.
2. Generated posts contain no proprietary information. Anyone can produce the
   same thing, so it builds no advantage.
3. For a company whose entire proposition is AI judgement, being visibly caught
   publishing generic AI output is worse than posting nothing.

The version worth building uses AI where it has an edge and keeps humans where
judgement and credibility live:

- **Listening and routing.** Lead Scout already researches public company
  information. Social signals are the same problem with a different source.
- **Atomising, not authoring.** A build log or decision record written by a
  person becomes platform-specific drafts. The source of truth is real work; the
  model reshapes rather than invents.
- **The inbox.** Answering first, capturing the brief properly, handing off to a
  person. This is where the revenue is in these markets.

## 4. The WhatsApp argument

WhatsApp reaches 96.5% of Nigerian internet users aged 16+ and 93% in Ghana. In
both markets it is not a chat app, it is the commerce layer. Orders, quotes,
negotiation and payment coordination happen there.

Aksen's own pitch is to deploy agents where customers already are, naming
WhatsApp first. The conclusion follows directly:

**Aksen's own funnel should terminate in WhatsApp, not a contact form.**

Two things happen at once. The funnel matches how people in these markets
actually behave, and every prospect conversation becomes a live demonstration of
the product. A buyer who messages and gets an agent that takes their brief
properly has already seen the thing being sold. The order and support
demonstrations on the site are most of this already.

This is the highest-leverage single move available.

## 5. Where I think the founder's thesis needs adjusting

The demographic case is real. Africa is projected to hold 42% of global youth by
2030, and sub-Saharan Africa accounts for around 90% of the growth in the
world's working-age population. That is as large as it sounds.

But it describes the talent pool and the consumer market of the 2030s. It does
not describe who signs a contract in 2026. The people with budget for a
GHS 3,000 to 50,000 engagement today are business owners between roughly 35 and
55. They are on WhatsApp and Facebook. They are not the youth cohort.

Aiming content at "African youth" produces an audience that cannot buy and an
inbox full of job applications.

The demographic dividend pays off now in exactly one way, and it is worth
pursuing deliberately: **hiring**. Content aimed at young technical talent is a
genuine asset, because that is how this company staffs its growth affordably and
well. It should be run as recruitment, labelled as recruitment, and measured as
recruitment. Not blended into a revenue story it cannot support.

The same caution applies to TikTok. The reach is real: 89.7% penetration in
Nigeria, second place in Ghana ahead of Facebook, and the largest advertising
reach of any platform there. But reach is not buyers. TikTok builds a personal
audience of young people. For selling operations software to SME owners, that is
an expensive detour. It earns one timeboxed experiment, not a pillar.

## 6. What social media can and cannot do in this market

Buying in Ghana and Nigeria at this size is relational and referral-led. Content
rarely closes. What it does is make you known and credible **before** the
referral arrives, so that when someone says "I know a person who does this," the
name is already familiar and the website already looks serious.

That changes the measure of success. Not "leads from Instagram" but "how often a
first conversation starts already trusting us." Expect attribution to stay
messy; plan for it rather than pretending it will be clean.

## 7. Honest weighing

**Good**

- Building in public is differentiated here and cheap for us specifically,
  because the raw material is a byproduct of work already done.
- First mover on the "agency that runs itself on AI" story in West Africa.
- Content doubles as sales collateral and as knowledge for the support
  assistant. One artifact, three uses.
- A real talent magnet, which is where the demographic tailwind pays today.
- It compounds. The blog exists; each piece raises the floor.

**Bad**

- Founder dependency. A personal voice carries early reach and does not
  transfer. If posting stops, the pipeline stops.
- Time. One person doing client work, product build and daily posting will drop
  one within about six weeks, and it is usually the content, or worse, the
  client work.
- Unqualified inbound has a real cost. AI content in these markets attracts
  students and job seekers in volume.
- Association with AI hype, which serious buyers discount.
- Publishing results we have not delivered would be fatal to trust, and the
  temptation grows when engagement is slow.

**Neutral or genuinely uncertain**

- LinkedIn in Ghana is thin, but it is where corporate and NGO budgets are
  reachable professionally. Unproven for us either way.
- Facebook has enormous SME reach in both markets, but weak organic B2B reach.
  Probably a paid channel later rather than an organic one now.
- Whether any of this produces clean attribution. It will not. Decide in advance
  what evidence would be good enough.

## 8. The foundation, in layers

Ordered so that each layer keeps working if the one above it is abandoned.

**Layer 0 — own the audience.** Email list and the blog. Resend is already
wired; the list does not exist yet. Platforms are rented land; this is the part
that survives an algorithm change or a lost account.

**Layer 1 — the proof engine.** One substantial build log per week, drawn from
work already done. Atomised into five to eight platform-native posts, drafted
with AI from the real artifact and edited by a person.

**Layer 2 — the conversation.** WhatsApp as the terminal call to action, agent
assisted, person finished. See section 4.

**Layer 3 — measurement.** The `opportunities` table already has a `source`
field and `prospect_leads` records provenance. Make attribution first-class now
while volume is zero and it costs nothing.

**Layer 4 — the product.** A single inbox across WhatsApp, Instagram and
Facebook, with an agent answering first and a content calendar drawn from real
business events. This is already noted as a monetisable idea.

## 9. The through-line

Social media is not a marketing function bolted onto Aksen. Every problem solved
for our own presence — one inbox across channels, agents answering first,
content generated from real events, attribution that survives contact with
reality — is a feature of the product described in
`Backend-As-Product-Observations.md`.

**Doing our own social media properly is product development that happens to
also generate demand.** That is what makes it worth the time, and it is the test
to apply to any tactic proposed later: if it does not also teach us something we
can sell, it is probably a distraction.

## 10. Sustainable cadence

Any plan requiring daily original creation will fail. This one is parasitic on
work already happening.

- Weekly: one build log, 60 to 90 minutes to shape from existing commits and
  documents.
- Weekly: atomise into posts. AI drafts, person edits.
- Daily: fifteen minutes replying to other people's posts. This is where B2B
  relationships actually form, and it is the part everyone skips.
- Monthly: one substantial piece with real numbers.

## 11. Open questions

- What is the first owned-audience offer worth an email address?
- Does the founder want a personal brand, an Aksen brand, or both? They need
  different content and have different exit properties.
- What is the honest position when a prospect asks for case studies before we
  have them?
- How much unqualified inbound is tolerable before the WhatsApp funnel needs a
  qualifying step?
