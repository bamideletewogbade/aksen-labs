# Outreach playbook

How to reach owners of local businesses, in person and online, and turn a stranger into a conversation, then a conversation into a free assessment, and a free assessment into a quoted build.

Written 27 September 2026 for Phase 1 of the operating brief: **20 logged owner conversations in four weeks**. Since 28 September the assessment is free for every business, so money comes up only after it, when there is a fix worth building. Every contact goes in the pipeline; every conversation goes in `Docs/Owner-Conversations.md` the same day.

---

## The one idea

**You are not selling on the first contact.** You are asking for a conversation about their orders. The sale happens in the second or third contact, after the owner has told you, in their own words, what the problem costs them.

Every contact has one job: get the next step.

```
stranger  ->  10-minute talk  ->  free assessment (find the bottleneck)  ->  quoted build
```

If you leave without a next step and a date, the contact did not happen.

---

## Who to reach

Four things you can check from outside, before you spend any time:

1. **Sells over WhatsApp or Instagram DMs.** Number on the signboard, "DM to order", a WhatsApp link in the bio.
2. **Owner-led.** The owner is in the shop or answers the phone. Staff can say no; only owners can say yes.
3. **Enough volume to hurt.** Posts often, visible stock, busy at weekends.
4. **No order system.** Orders are confirmed in the chat. "DM for price" is a good sign.

**Best fits:** boutiques, cosmetics and skincare, cakes and pastries, catering and small chops, decor and furniture, phones and accessories, printing, tailoring and anything made to order.

**Skip:** chains and franchises (the owner is not there), market stalls with no phone orders, anyone already using an ordering tool.

---

## Before you contact anyone: the two-minute look

Do this for every name on the list. It is what makes your opener specific instead of generic.

- **Send a real enquiry to their WhatsApp in the evening.** Something you would actually buy, or a gift. Note how long the reply takes and what they have to ask you. If you mention this later, say plainly it was you.
- **Read their Google reviews** for "no reply", "didn't respond", "wrong order", "never delivered".
- **Check Instagram comments** for "price?" or "how much?" left unanswered.
- **Note one specific product you genuinely like.**

Write what you find next to their name. It becomes your first sentence.

## In person

### Where and when

Work one area per outing so you can visit 8 to 10 shops in two hours. Osu (Oxford Street and the side streets), East Legon (Lagos Avenue, around A&C), Spintex Road, Madina, Adabraka, Dzorwulu and Airport Residential for higher-end shops.

**Go Tuesday to Thursday, 10:00 to 12:00 or 14:00 to 16:00.** Avoid Monday mornings (restocking), Saturdays (their best selling day) and the busy days around month-end salaries. If a customer walks in while you are talking, step aside and wait. That courtesy is noticed.

### What to bring

- A small card: your name, your WhatsApp number, one line ("I help shops that sell on WhatsApp keep their orders organised").
- A notebook. Writing by hand looks like listening. Typing on a phone looks like texting.
- Your phone, with `/order-demo` ready. Show it only if they ask what it looks like.
- Smart casual. Neat, not a suit.

### The visit, step by step

**1. Greet properly and take your time.** "Good morning, how are you?" Wait for the answer. A Twi greeting if you have it. Skipping the greeting reads as rude, and rude ends the visit before it starts.

**2. Ask for the owner.** "Please, is the owner around?" If not: "When is the best time to catch them?" and ask the name. Do not pitch the staff. Leave a card and come back at the time they gave you. Returning exactly when you said you would is itself a reason to trust you.

**3. Say who you are and what you want, in one breath.**

> "My name is Bishop. I build order systems for shops that sell on WhatsApp. I'm not selling anything today. I'm talking to shop owners around here about how they handle their orders. Can I ask you three quick questions?"

**4. Ask, do not explain.**

- "How do most of your orders come in now?"
- "Tell me about the last really busy day. What happened with the messages?"
- "When did an order last go wrong, a mix-up or a customer who gave up waiting? What happened?"

Then stop talking. When something sounds painful, go one level deeper: "What did that cost you?" "What did you do about it?" "How often does that happen?"

**5. Ask for the next step before you leave.** If the problem is real:

> "Can I come back one afternoon this week and sit with you for twenty minutes? I'll watch how the orders come in and tell you honestly whether there's a fix worth paying for."

Get a day and a time. Then: "Let me send you a message now so you have my number," and send it while you are standing there.

**6. Leave within ten minutes** unless they keep you talking. Short first visits get second visits.

**Between shops, two minutes:** their exact words, one line on the problem, the next step and its date.

### What they will say

| They say | You say |
| --- | --- |
| "We're fine, we manage." | "Good. How do you manage on a busy Saturday?" Do not argue. Ask about a specific day. |
| "How much is it?" | "It depends on what's actually going wrong, and I don't know that yet. That's what the twenty minutes is for." If pressed: "Looking at it costs you nothing. If there's something worth building, I'll tell you what it costs before you decide anything." |
| "Send me something on WhatsApp." | Usually a polite no. "Sure, I'll send it now while I'm here. Can I call you Thursday to hear what you think?" |
| "My nephew does our tech." | "Great. What does he handle? What's still on you?" |
| "Come back later." | "When exactly?" Get a day. |
| "This is a great idea!" | Thank them, then ask for the twenty minutes. Praise is not interest. Time is. |

## Online

### Where to find them

- **Instagram:** search Accra plus the category, and hashtags such as #accravendor, #ghanavendors, #accrabakers, #madeinghana. Look for "DM to order" and unanswered "price?" comments.
- **Google Maps:** "boutique Spintex", "cake shop East Legon". Keep the ones with a phone number, recent reviews, and complaints about slow replies.
- **TikTok:** vendors doing live sales or "order now" videos.
- **Jiji and Tonaton:** sellers who also have a physical shop.
- **Your own contacts' WhatsApp Status.** A lot of people you already know run a business on the side. Your contact list is a list.

**Use the channel they sell on.** WhatsApp in the bio, message WhatsApp. "DM to order", DM them.

### The first message

Short. About them. One question. No link, no price, no deck.

**Instagram DM**

> "Hi, I've been looking at your [specific product], it's lovely. Quick question from someone who builds order tools for shops here: when a post like this goes out and the DMs flood in, how do you keep track of who ordered what?"

**WhatsApp, to the business number**

> "Good afternoon. My name is Bishop, I'm in Accra and I help shops that sell on WhatsApp keep their orders organised. I found you on [Instagram / Google]. I'm not selling anything, I'm asking shop owners one question: what happens to the messages that come in while you're busy or closed?"

**Voice note, same words, 30 to 40 seconds.** Here a warm voice note from a real person often gets answered when text is ignored. Record it standing up, not reading. Re-record if you sound like a script.

### When they reply

Continue the conversation using the in-person questions. After two or three exchanges, move off text:

> "Easier to show than to type. Can I call you tomorrow at 11 for fifteen minutes, or pass by the shop?"

### Follow-up: three messages, then stop

- **Day 0:** the first message.
- **Day 3:** one line, a new angle. "Saw your new [product] post. Still curious how you handle the DMs after a drop like that."
- **Day 10:** "I'll leave it here. If orders ever get messy, I'm on this number."

Then stop. Never a fourth.

### Rules

- One person at a time, written or spoken by you. No broadcasts, no adding people to groups, no auto-DM tools. WhatsApp bans numbers that get reported as spam, and your number is your business.
- If someone says stop, stop, and mark it in the pipeline.
- Note where you found every number. The Data Protection Act 2012 (Act 843) applies to personal numbers.

## Multipliers: people who already visit shops

Once the basic rhythm is running, these reach many owners through one relationship:

- **Delivery riders and dispatch companies.** They know exactly which shops send many orders a day.
- **Packaging suppliers, MoMo merchant agents, POS vendors, sign printers.** They are in these shops every week.
- **Associations and rooms:** trade and market associations, church business fellowships, Ghana Enterprises Agency trainings, business meetups.
- **Every owner you talk to.** End every conversation, including the ones that say no, with: "Who else do you know who deals with this?"

## The week

Target from the brief: 20 conversations in four weeks, so about five a week.

| Day | Work |
| --- | --- |
| Monday, 1 hour | Build the list: 15 new names, two-minute look done on each. |
| Tuesday and Wednesday morning | Walk one area, 8 to 10 shops each day. |
| Tuesday and Wednesday afternoon | 10 first messages online, plus every follow-up that is due. |
| Thursday | Hold the booked diagnostics and calls. |
| Friday, 30 minutes | Write up, update the pipeline, record the two numbers. |

**What counts as a conversation:** ten minutes or more in which an owner talked about their own orders. "Not interested" is not a conversation. A visit where only staff were there is not a conversation.

**Expect most first contacts to go nowhere.** Most DMs go unanswered and most walk-ins are short. That is normal and it is not information about the offer. After two weeks, count your own rates (walk-ins to conversations, messages to replies) and plan from those, not from anyone's averages.

## From conversation to money

**The free assessment.** "Bring me one thing about your orders that annoys you." If you can, sit in the shop and watch messages arrive; on a call, share your screen and run it from **Assessments** in the workspace, which walks the questions and writes down their answers. Map how an order moves from the first message to delivery, find where they get lost, and name the smallest fix worth doing, including anything they can do themselves this week. It costs them nothing. Ask the pricing question from R-005 in every one:

> "If two people offered you this, one at GHS 10,000 once and GHS 4,000 a month, and one at GHS 1,200 a month with nothing to pay today, which would you pick, and why?"

**Money comes up at the end, and only if the problem is real and a fix is worth building:**

> "Here is what I found, and here is the smallest fix I think is worth doing. Some of it you can do yourself. If you want us to build the rest, I'll write you a quote, and you decide. Would that help?"

Then stop talking and let them answer.

**How to tell if someone is serious.** They give you time, they introduce you to someone, or they pay. Compliments, "send me a proposal" and "let me think about it" are not signals. Ask for a date for the thinking.
