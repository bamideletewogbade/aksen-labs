# Academy Suites | visit preparation and rehearsal

Private preparation for Bishop, Aksen Labs. Prepared 19 October 2026.

**Meeting source:** User-supplied meeting identity; not Calendar-verified. This is preparation for an intended visit. Date, time and attendees are not confirmed.

## The purpose

You have something concrete to show. Your job is to find out whether management sees a business reason to pay for it, identify who decides and agree the next action. You do not have to secure a sale before leaving for the visit to be useful.

Best outcome: they confirm a need and agree to review a bounded written proposal with the approver. Useful fallback: a named decision-maker and a specific return appointment. A clear no with a reason is better information than an indefinite 'send something'.

## Before you walk in

Charge the laptop and phone, bring the charger and data, open the preview and rehearse its exact path once. Keep a few demo screenshots or a screen recording available as backup. Screenshots show the design, not working integrations. Keep private prices and client records off the presentation screen.

The laptop preview is http://localhost:4317/ while the local server runs. Do not assume that address works on your phone. Use future dates supported by the demo; old suggested dates in the original pitch are not promises. The old brief's suggested 12:30 arrival is not a verified quiet period or appointment.

Take a minute outside. Let your shoulders settle, breathe at a comfortable pace, and read this: **I have prepared something worth discussing. I can ask clear questions, listen, and agree a next step. I do not need to know everything before starting.**

Your cash needs matter to your planning. Their willingness to buy depends on their priorities and confidence in delivery. Keep those two things separate while you speak.

## At reception

“Good afternoon. I'm Bishop from Aksen Labs. I prepared a short website concept for Academy Suites, focused on helping guests find the Old Ife Road branch and enquire about rooms or events. Who would be the right person to show it to?”

If asked whether you have an appointment: “I don't have one booked. If now is inconvenient, when would be a suitable time for a short introduction?” Use that answer only if it remains true.

If the manager is unavailable, ask for their role/name and the best way and time to arrange a meeting. Respect a refusal. Do not claim an introduction or personal connection you have not been given.

## A proposed 15-minute conversation

1. **Minutes 0–2: establish relevance.** “I have a short concept to show you. Before that, how do most room and event enquiries reach this branch today?”
2. **Minutes 2–6: understand the current process.** Ask what happens after an enquiry arrives, what staff repeatedly clarify, and whether anything needs improvement. Follow their answer rather than racing through a checklist.
3. **Minutes 6–9: demonstrate one guest journey.** Show the relevant path below. Pause and ask what would or would not fit reception's work.
4. **Minutes 9–12: discuss the buying conditions.** Find out who maintains the site, who approves work, whether there is a budget, and what timing matters.
5. **Minutes 12–15: agree a next step.** Repeat their priority in their words and propose the smallest useful scope. Agree who reviews the proposal and a specific date for the discussion.

## Your two-minute demonstration

> “Imagine a guest planning a trip to Ibadan. They can see which branch this is, explore the rooms, and prepare an enquiry with their dates and guest count. These availability results and prices are examples for the demonstration. Reception would still confirm a real booking.”

Show the home page, choose dates and guests, select a sample room, then show the reviewed message. Say: “Reception gets a clearer starting request. Would this fit how your staff work?” Stop before sending anything.

If events matter, show the different information an event enquiry asks for: date, space and attendance. Finish on the branch directions or the page they care about. Do not tour every page.

If they ask about live availability: “That would depend on your current booking system and the access it provides. I'd check that with your provider before including it in a quote.”

Do not lead by declaring the existing site broken. Historical research found specific defects on 14–15 October, but those have not been reproduced in this session. If you can reproduce one together, show it calmly and ask whether their existing provider can fix it.

## Introducing the other work

“We also build simple tools for handling enquiries and follow-ups. If there is a part of that process that is difficult for your team, I'd like to understand it before recommending anything.”

Use one relevant example: a shared enquiry list if staff lose track of follow-up; an event enquiry and quotation process if event details are scattered; monthly care if nobody owns updates. Leave AI, dashboards and integrations until there is a clear reason to discuss them.

## When they ask about money

The old pitch contains NGN 450,000 for the Front Desk website, NGN 180,000 for a broader Trust Fix package and NGN 45,000 monthly care. They are internal draft figures. Decide which, if any, you are prepared to stand behind after checking scope and delivery effort.

If you choose to use the existing website figure: “For the focused website and enquiry scope we've discussed, my indicative fee is NGN 450,000, subject to confirming the content, access and final deliverables. Live booking integration would be separate. I'll put the scope and payment stages in writing.” Do not use this sentence unless you have made that pricing decision.

If you are not ready to name a fee: “I want to give you a fixed price against exactly what you need. Once we've confirmed the pages, enquiry process and access, I'll send a clear scope and fee. Is there a budget range I should work within?”

Ask, then allow them time to answer. Do not offer a discount to fill a silence. If the budget is smaller, reduce scope deliberately rather than promising the whole project for less.

## Calm responses to common objections

| They say | Your response |
| --- | --- |
| We already have a website. | “Yes. This concept explores a clearer branch and enquiry experience. Is there anything about the current site or enquiry process you would actually like improved?” |
| We already have a web person. | “Good. I'd want to understand what they handle and work with them where appropriate. Is there any work you need that isn't covered today?” |
| How many extra bookings will we get? | “I can't promise a number. We can agree the improvements and measure enquiries and reception-confirmed outcomes if you want to assess the result.” |
| This is expensive. | “Which part is most valuable to you, and what budget are you considering? We can look at a smaller first scope.” |
| Send a proposal. | “Certainly. Which outcome should it focus on, who else needs to review it, and when can we discuss it?” |
| No budget now. | “Understood. Is this something you expect to budget for later, or is it simply not a priority?” |
| Are you finished with the website already? | “This is a private concept. A real version needs your approved information and photos, agreed scope, testing and handover.” |

## The close

“From what you've described, the priority is [their actual issue]. I suggest [small agreed scope]. Who should review the written proposal with you, and what day works to discuss it?”

If they are ready to proceed, move to a written scope, fee, payment stages and acceptance criteria. Do not promise a delivery date before checking dependencies and your capacity.

## Rehearse out loud once

Say your reception introduction in under 30 seconds. Answer “We already have a website” without criticising their provider. Demonstrate the guest journey in two minutes, including the sample-data explanation. Practise the price answer you have chosen. Finish by asking for a named person and dated next action.

If you stumble, pause and say the next sentence more simply. You are having a conversation with a business owner, not performing a memorised speech. Speak a little slower than your nerves want you to, and let them finish.

## Immediately afterwards

Spend five minutes recording the person's name/role, their exact problem, approver, budget indication, dependencies and next step. Record 'unknown' where you did not learn something. A follow-up draft can then repeat what was actually agreed; sending remains Bishop's action.
