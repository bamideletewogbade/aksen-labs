## The single most important fact

**All three accounts route to the same WhatsApp number: +234 816 857 2880.**

Operationally this is already one business with one inbox and three shopfronts. The bios also cross-link each other, so Ope already treats them as a family rather than as separate companies.

## What does not exist

Searched for a website, an online store, a Google Business listing, directory entries and marketplace listings. Found none for any of the three brands.

- No website on any of the three profiles. The only link is the WhatsApp deep link.
- Not indexed under the business name, the address, or the phone number.
- No Jiji, Finelib, HotFrog or InfoIsInfo listing found.

So roughly 62,000 followers across three accounts, and every one of them has exactly one route to buy: message a WhatsApp number and wait for a human. There is no owned channel, no catalogue that survives the scroll, and no record of an enquiry that is not a chat thread.

That is the gap, and it is the exact shape of what Aksen says it does.

## Audience concentration

@theframe_shop holds about 87 percent of the total following and carries the verified badge. The other two accounts are small by comparison. Any domain, brand name and search effort should sit under The Frame Shop. The equity is there and it is not close.

## Competitor read

**Framed In Lagos** (framedinlagos.com, RC 1378513, Ikeja) is the most developed competitor found.

- Combines Custom Framing, Artworks and Interiors in one storefront, not three.
- Real e-commerce: cart, add to cart, free shipping over N100k, quoted delivery of one to two days.
- Entry prices advertised as framing from N6,999, artworks from N9,999, interiors from N9,999.

Other established Lagos players in the same space (HOG, Eunicon, Eloquent Displays, Decor.ng) also combine decor categories under one site.

Market norm is combined. Nobody serious is running three separate sites.

## Pricing signal from Ope's own posts

A public @theframe_shop reel quotes a 36x70 inch frame at **N85,000**.

Set against Framed In Lagos entry pricing, Ope appears to be selling at the premium end rather than competing on entry price. Treat this as one data point from one post, not a price list.

## One site or three

**Recommendation: one website, one brand, two journeys.**

Combine. Do not build three sites, and do not build one flat catalogue either.

Why combine:

1. One WhatsApp number already means one operation. Three sites would model a structure the business does not actually have.
2. The nearest real competitor combines, and so does everyone else with a functioning site. Splitting would be a deliberate bet against the whole market.
3. Same buyer. Somebody choosing a wall frame for a living room is the person who buys the ornament and the vase. Cross-sell is the point, and three sites throw it away.
4. They have zero organic search presence today. One domain building authority beats three thin domains competing with each other.
5. Aksen has to maintain whatever gets built. Three sites is triple the ongoing cost for no extra revenue.

Why not one flat catalogue: the two buyers are genuinely different, and that difference is visible in their own highlights.

- **Hotels, short-lets and interior designers.** Signalled by "elegant homes and hotels" and the Installations and Prices highlights. Wants a portfolio, evidence of past installs, and a quote conversation. High value, slow, relationship led.
- **Individual decor buyers.** Signalled by "Decor Haul", Best sellers, Ornaments, Flowers. Wants to browse, see a price, and order. Lower value, fast, impulse led.

Proposed shape:

- One domain under The Frame Shop.
- A **Shop** path for decor, art and ornaments. Browse, price, order.
- A **Projects** path for bespoke, commissioned and hospitality work. Portfolio, installations, request a quote.
- @decors_store and @tfs_gallery become sections inside that site, not separate properties. They stay as Instagram accounts if Ope wants the separate feeds. Keeping three feeds and one site is fine and probably right.

## The pushback: do not start with a checkout

The obvious move is to copy Framed In Lagos and build an online store with a cart. Argue against that for phase one.

This is a WhatsApp native business. Every account already funnels to one number, the products need sizing conversations and custom work, delivery is negotiated, and payment in this market commonly settles by transfer. Bolting on a checkout means a payment gateway, a returns policy, stock accuracy and reconciliation, and it fights the sales motion that is already producing revenue.

Better phase one: a catalogue that ends in a WhatsApp handoff carrying the product context with it, so Ope's team opens a thread that already says which piece, which size and which price the customer was looking at. Then the enquiry gets recorded rather than lost in a chat backlog.

That is Aksen's actual product, applied to a real business, and it can be measured: enquiries captured, reply time, and how many turn into orders. Earn the checkout later once there is a reason to believe it.

## Flags to raise

1. **Nigeria, not Ghana.** Aksen's blueprint, launch research and indicative pricing are all Ghana first and quoted in GHS. This client is in Lagos, buying in NGN. That affects pricing, payment, delivery assumptions and any local claim the positioning makes. Worth settling deliberately rather than by accident, because client one tends to define the company.
2. **Outside the stated beachhead.** The decision ledger says start with property and service businesses. This is retail and home decor. A paying client beats staying pure, but if the second and third clients also come from decor, that is the real market speaking and the ledger should be updated rather than ignored.
3. **Possible name collision.** A separate account, @theframeshop.ng, exists and describes itself as The Frame Shop in Lagos. Unclear whether it belongs to Ope, is a dormant handle, or is somebody else. Matters before choosing a domain and before any trademark or naming work. Ask her.
4. **Personal relationship, commercial engagement.** Warm intro from school, and the reply so far is friendly rather than commercial. Nothing has been scoped or priced. Treat the first conversation as discovery, not as a close.
