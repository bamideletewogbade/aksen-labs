# Discovery call guide: Ope

Run this from top to bottom. Target 40 minutes. The questions from `discovery-questions.md` are folded in here in the order that actually works on a call, with the ones that do not change the scope cut out.

The goal is not to sell. The goal is to leave with three numbers and one named problem.

---

## Before you dial (2 minutes)

Have open: this guide, and `discovery-brief.md` for the facts.

Know these already, so you never ask her something you could have looked up:

- Three accounts, one WhatsApp number, 60 Shipeolu Street, Palmgrove
- @theframe_shop has about 53.9K followers and the verified badge, the other two are under 5K each
- No website, no Google listing, nothing indexed anywhere
- A recent reel prices a 36x70 frame at N85,000

Decide before you start: you are not quoting anything on this call.

---

## 1. Opening (3 minutes)

Warm first, then ask permission to switch gears. The permission ask is what stops it feeling like an ambush.

> "Good to see the business doing well. I have been watching the frames page for a while, it looks serious now."

Then:

> "So you know I build software. I have started something on my own doing AI workflows for businesses, and honestly when I saw your setup I thought there was something obvious we could fix for you. Can I ask you some nosy questions about how the business actually runs? Not selling you anything today, I just want to understand it properly first."

**Wait for a yes before continuing.** If she says yes, she has agreed to a working conversation and the rest is easy.

If she deflects or seems busy, ask for a specific slot instead: "Give me thirty minutes next week and I will come with something useful."

---

## 2. Volume and the inbox (8 minutes)

This block gives you the size of the problem. Everything else depends on it.

> "Take a normal week. Roughly how many people message that WhatsApp number?"

**Write the number down.** If she does not know, ask her to open WhatsApp and look at yesterday.

> "And who is actually answering them? You, or someone else too?"

> "When somebody first messages, what do they usually ask before they ask for a price?"

*Listen for: the same three or four questions repeating. That repetition is the thing worth automating, and she will name it without realising.*

> "How fast does a first reply usually go out? And what happens to the ones that come in at 11pm or on a Sunday?"

**Write the reply time down.**

> "Does it ever happen that a message just gets missed, or two people answer the same person?"

> "How often, would you say? Once a week? Every day?"

*This is the money question. If she says it happens often, you have a quantifiable loss and a reason to act. If she says never, believe her and move on, do not push it.*

---

## 3. From message to money (10 minutes)

> "Walk me through one order that happened recently. From the first message all the way to the customer having it on their wall."

**Let her talk. Do not interrupt.** This single answer usually replaces five questions. Note where she hesitates or says "then I have to check", because those are the gaps.

Then fill in whatever she skipped:

> "When somebody asks for a price, what do you need to know before you can answer? Size, room, finish?"

> "After the chat, where does the order actually get written down?"

*Listen for: notebook, her head, a WhatsApp thread she scrolls back through. Anything other than a system is an opening.*

> "How does payment usually happen, and at what point?"

> "And when it is a custom piece rather than something you have in stock, how different is that?"

---

## 4. The three brands (5 minutes)

> "The frame shop, the gallery and the decor store. In your head, is that one business or three?"

> "Do the same people buy across all three, or are they different customers?"

> "If a customer had to say your business name out loud, which one do you want them saying?"

*You are testing the recommendation in the brief. If she says one business and names The Frame Shop, the one site with two journeys is confirmed. If she is emotionally attached to three separate identities, do not fight her on the call. Note it and think about it after.*

Quick factual one, slip it in:

> "Is @theframeshop.ng you?"

---

## 5. Which customer matters (5 minutes)

> "Roughly how much of the money comes from hotels, short-lets and interior designers, versus regular people buying for their own house?"

**Write the split down.**

> "And which of those two would you rather have more of next year?"

*Her answer here decides what gets built first. Hotels means portfolio and quote flow. Individuals means catalogue and fast reply.*

> "For the hotel and designer work, how does it normally start? Somebody refers you, or they just DM?"

---

## 6. What has already been tried (4 minutes)

> "Has there ever been a website, or a shop on Jiji, anything like that?"

*If yes, find out what happened to it. A dead website is important context and slightly awkward, so ask gently.*

> "Are you running ads, or is it all organic?"

---

## 7. The real question (3 minutes)

Ask this one slowly and then be quiet.

> "What is the one thing about running this that most makes you want to throw your phone across the room?"

*This is the most valuable question on the list. Whatever she names is the thing to build first, regardless of what you walked in thinking. Write it down in her exact words.*

---

## 8. Close (2 minutes)

> "Who else touches the business day to day? Would they use something if we built it?"

> "Last one. If we did something together, what would have to be true in three months for you to say it was worth it?"

**Write that down verbatim.** It is your success measure and later it is your case study.

Then set the next step and end:

> "Let me go away and write up what I heard, and come back with one specific thing worth fixing first and what it would take. Give me until [name a day]. If it does not look worth it I will tell you that too."

Naming a day matters. Naming the possibility of "not worth it" is what makes you credible rather than another person selling a website.

---

## If she asks "how much?"

She probably will. Do not improvise a number.

> "Honestly I do not know yet, and if I threw a figure at you now it would be wrong in both directions. Let me understand the work first, then I will give you a real number with what is in it. If it is not worth the money I will say so."

Then go straight back to your next question. Do not let it sit.

---

## If she says "just build me a website"

> "I might. But a website that just sits there will not fix the messages piling up on WhatsApp, and that is the part that is actually costing you. Let me look at both and tell you which one earns its money first."

---

## Do not do

- Do not quote a price, or hint at a range.
- Do not promise a website.
- Do not describe anything Aksen has already built and run for a client, because there is not one yet.
- Do not lean on the school connection to close. Use it to get an honest answer to the phone-throwing question.
- Do not fill silences. After the big questions, count to five.

---

Three numbers:

3. Hospitality versus individual revenue split: ______

One sentence, in her words, naming the thing that most frustrates her: 

______________________________________________

One sentence, in her words, on what makes this worth it in three months:

______________________________________________

---

## Within an hour of hanging up

Write it up while it is fresh. Add a `call-notes-YYYY-MM-DD.md` to this folder with:

- The three numbers
- Her two verbatim sentences
- Anything in `discovery-brief.md` that turned out to be wrong, corrected
- The one workflow you would scope, and roughly what it would take

Then decide whether there is a real engagement here before writing a proposal. If the volume is low and nothing is actually breaking, the honest answer is that she does not need this yet, and saying so is worth more than a small bad first client.
