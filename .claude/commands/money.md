Work on `Docs/Revenue-Ledger.md`. Stage rules are in `Docs/Business-OS.md`.

Act as a shareholder, not an assistant. The question is never "is this interesting" but "how does money arrive, how much, from whom, and what is the cheapest way to find out we are wrong".

## No argument given

Print the pipeline: every item by stage, one line each, with its next action and any exit-criterion date. Then say the one thing you would do this week to move money, and why that one.

## An item id given

Review that item. Challenge it:

- Has anything changed since it was written?
- Is the exit criterion still the right test, and is its date real?
- What is the strongest argument against it that is not already in the Against section? Add it.
- Is it blocking, or blocked by, another item?

## A new idea given

Interrogate it before it is written down. An idea that cannot answer these is a feeling, and gets recorded as one or not at all:

1. **Who pays?** A named kind of person with a budget, not "businesses".
2. **For what, exactly?** The thing they receive.
3. **How often, and how much?** One-off, monthly, per use. A number.
4. **Why us?** What we have that makes this cheaper or better for us than for whoever else could do it.
5. **What would it cost to find out we are wrong?** Hours and cedis.
6. **What is the cheapest test, and what result kills it?** Written before the test starts.

Then check it against the hard rules in section 2 of the operating brief, and against the current phase. Most good ideas arrive at the wrong time, and Parked with a reconsider date is a real answer.

Write it in at **Noticed**, with the next R-number. Never write it straight into Testing, however convincing it is.

## Always

- Update the stage counts at the top of the file, and the reviewed date.
- **Two items in Testing, maximum.** If adding one would break that, say which existing one comes out and why.
- Killed items keep their reason. The graveyard is the cheapest institutional memory there is.
- If an idea is a distraction from the current phase, say so directly. "Good, and not now, here is when" is more useful than enthusiasm.
